# Hwy4Events — Design Personas

7 canonical personas representing the core audience for Hwy4Events. Used to guide product decisions, feature prioritization, and content strategy.

---

## 1. "Gary" — The Plugged-In Retiree

**Demographics:** 68, retired teacher, lives full-time in Arnold with his wife. Moved up from Sacramento 6 years ago.

**Tech comfort:** Comfortable with iPhone, checks email and Facebook daily. Doesn't love apps — prefers mobile web. Will bookmark a site but won't download anything.

**Current event discovery:** Gary already knows what's happening. He's on the Moose Lodge email list, reads the Calaveras Enterprise, sees flyers at the post office and Snowshoe Brewery, and hears about everything at the Arnold Byway Cafe. His wife is in two Facebook groups.

**Relationship to Hwy4Events:** Supplemental. Gary doesn't *need* the site — he's already wired into the grapevine. But he checks it to catch things outside his usual orbit (a new band at Murphys, a festival in Copperopolis he wouldn't have heard about). He also likes seeing the full picture in one place rather than piecing it together from 5 sources.

**Jobs to be done:**
- "What's happening this week that I might be missing?"
- Quickly scan by town or category — skip the stuff he already knows
- Forward a link to his buddy: "Hey, did you see this?"

**Pain points:**
- Doesn't want to create an account or get notifications
- If the site is slow or hard to read on his phone, he'll just ask his wife
- Annoyed by events listed with wrong times or missing details

**Design implications:**
- Fast, scannable list view is essential — Gary won't scroll forever
- Share functionality matters (text/email a link)
- Accuracy and freshness of data build trust with this persona
- Don't gate anything behind sign-up

## 2. "Mia" — The Young Winery Worker

**Demographics:** 27, tasting room associate at a Murphys winery. Lives in a rental in Murphys. Single, moved up from Fresno 2 years ago.

**Tech comfort:** High. Instagram-first. Comfortable with any app or site.

**Current event discovery:** Instagram (follows Murphys venues, Mystic Saloon, local bands), word of mouth at work, and the winery's own event calendar. Doesn't read the local paper or use Facebook much.

**Relationship to Hwy4Events:** Primary discovery tool for things outside her Murphys bubble. She knows what's happening on Main Street but has no idea what's going on in Arnold, Copperopolis, or Bear Valley. Also uses it professionally — guests at the tasting room ask "what else should we do this weekend?" and she wants a quick answer.

**Jobs to be done:**
- "What's fun this weekend?" (social — looking for nightlife, live music, festivals)
- "What should I tell tasting room guests to do tonight?"
- Discover events in towns she doesn't usually visit

**Pain points:**
- If the site looks dated or "boomer-ish," she won't trust it
- Wants to see the vibe — photos, descriptions that sound human, not robotic
- Hates clicking through 10 things to find the one live music event

**Design implications:**
- Category filtering (especially "Live Music") is high-value for Mia
- Visual quality and brand voice matter — she's comparing this to Instagram
- Mobile-first — she's checking this on her phone at work or at a bar
- "Tonight" or "This Weekend" quick filters are her entry point

## 3. "Dave" — The Dorrington Contractor

**Demographics:** 45, runs a small contracting business out of Dorrington. Wife, no kids at home (teenagers are mostly independent). Has lived in the corridor his whole life.

**Tech comfort:** Moderate. Uses his phone for work (texts, maps, photos of job sites). Doesn't browse much for fun.

**Current event discovery:** Hears about things from clients and at the hardware store. His wife handles the social calendar. He'll go to car shows, BBQ competitions, community fundraisers, and lodge events. Not interested in wine tastings or "artsy stuff."

**Relationship to Hwy4Events:** Dave himself won't check the site regularly — but his wife might. When he does look, it's because she said "check if there's anything going on Saturday" and he needs a fast answer.

**Jobs to be done:**
- "Is there anything happening this Saturday?" (quick yes/no scan)
- Find the specific type of thing he likes (car shows, outdoor events, BBQ, community events)
- His wife sends him a link and he needs it to load fast and make sense

**Pain points:**
- Won't scroll past the first screen if nothing grabs him
- Doesn't care about descriptions or "editorial" content — just: what, when, where
- If the site asks him to do anything (sign up, allow notifications, accept cookies), he's gone

**Design implications:**
- Zero-friction access — no modals, no popups, no gates
- Dense, information-rich event cards (name, date, town, category — at a glance)
- Weekend view is his primary lens
- "Community" category helps him find his kind of events

## 4. "Rob" — The Bay Area Weekend Visitor

**Demographics:** 48, lives in Los Gatos. CSO at a media tech company. Goes up to the Hwy 4 corridor about once a month for weekends, usually with wife Vivian. Has a place or stays with friends.

**Tech comfort:** Very high. Power user. Built this site.

**Current event discovery:** Before: Vivian checking Facebook, flyers at the Lube Room, Instagram (Mystic Saloon etc.), Moose Lodge emails, stumbling onto things in Murphys. Now: Hwy4Events is his primary source.

**Relationship to Hwy4Events:** Rob is the creator and a power user. He checks it before each trip to plan the weekend. He also uses it to validate that the product is working — are events showing up? Is the briefing good? Are there gaps?

**Jobs to be done:**
- "What's happening *this* weekend?" (planning a specific upcoming trip)
- "Is there anything worth going up for next weekend?" (deciding whether to make the drive)
- Share events with Vivian: "Look at this, we should go"
- Validate site quality, freshness, and coverage

**Pain points:**
- Events that are stale, duplicated, or have wrong info erode his confidence
- Wants to see the "highlight reel" — not scroll through 40 events to find 3 good ones
- Needs the site to be impressive enough to share with friends ("check out this site for when you go to Arnold")

**Design implications:**
- "Rob's Picks" / editorial curation is high value for this persona (and he curates it)
- The daily briefing ("This Week on the 4") is built for this use case
- Weekend-focused view is primary
- Share/social features matter — Rob is an evangelist for the site
- Quality bar is high — this is also his portfolio piece

## 5. "Karen" — The Absentee Airbnb Owner

**Demographics:** 52, lives in Walnut Creek. Owns 4 Airbnb units in Arnold. Visits quarterly to check on properties but manages remotely. Former real estate agent.

**Tech comfort:** High for business tools (Airbnb app, property management software, Canva). Moderate for general web.

**Current event discovery:** Doesn't actively track events — that's the problem. She knows about Bear Valley ski season and the big Murphys festivals, but can't tell guests about the Thursday jazz night or the Saturday farmers market.

**Relationship to Hwy4Events:** Karen is a B2B-ish user — she doesn't consume events for herself, she redistributes them to guests. She wants a reliable, always-current source she can point guests to, or pull from for her welcome guides and pre-arrival messages.

**Jobs to be done:**
- Send guests a link that shows "here's what's happening during your stay"
- Copy/paste a few event highlights into her Airbnb welcome message
- Look credible and knowledgeable about the area (competitive advantage for her listings)

**Pain points:**
- If the site looks amateur, she won't share it with guests
- Needs date-range filtering: "What's happening March 14-16?"
- Stale data is deadly — if a guest shows up to a cancelled event, that's a bad review

**Design implications:**
- Date-range or "this weekend" filtering is critical for Karen's workflow
- The site must look polished enough to share with paying guests
- A shareable URL that shows a specific date range would be ideal
- The daily briefing could be something she forwards to guests directly
- "Welcome guide" integration is a future opportunity (embeddable widget, PDF export)

## 6. "Jen" — The Blue Lake Springs Mom

**Demographics:** 38, full-time resident in Arnold. Blue Lake Springs member. Husband works remotely. Kids ages 4 and 9. Originally from the Bay Area — moved up during COVID.

**Tech comfort:** High. iPhone power user. Active on Facebook groups, Nextdoor, and the BLS community app/portal.

**Current event discovery:** BLS newsletter and Facebook group for member events (pool parties, holiday events, kids' activities). For non-BLS events: Facebook groups, word of mouth at school pickup, and flyers at Sierra Hills Market.

**Relationship to Hwy4Events:** Jen is looking for kid-friendly activities beyond the BLS bubble. She plans her week ahead of time — usually Sunday night on her laptop after the kids are in bed, or scanning her phone during soccer practice. BLS member events are important to her but currently not on the site.

**Jobs to be done:**
- "What can I do with my kids this weekend?" (family-friendly filter)
- Keep track of BLS member events alongside public events
- Find activities that work for a 4-year-old AND a 9-year-old (broad age range)
- Plan a week of activities to keep kids entertained during school breaks

**Pain points:**
- Events without clear family-friendliness info are useless — she won't gamble with a 4-year-old
- Hates discovering events after they've passed: "Why didn't I know about this?"
- Needs to know logistics: Is it outdoors? Is there parking? Is it stroller-accessible?
- If BLS events aren't on the site, it's missing a big chunk of her calendar

**Design implications:**
- "Family" or "Kids" category/tag would be high-value
- BLS member events (tagged as "Members Only") could differentiate the site
- Week-ahead view matters for the planning parent
- Event descriptions should include practical details (venue type, outdoor/indoor, accessibility)
- Newsletter/email digest could be killer for this persona — she wants to be told, not to search

## 7. "Miguel" — The Central Valley Day-Tripper

**Demographics:** 34, lives in Stockton with his girlfriend. Works in logistics. Likes getting out of the valley on weekends — rotates between the coast, Tahoe, and the Gold Country / Hwy 4 corridor.

**Tech comfort:** High. Google Maps + Yelp are his planning tools. Discovers things via Instagram reels and TikTok.

**Current event discovery:** Mostly spontaneous. Might see a reel about a Murphys wine walk or a Bear Valley event and decide to drive up. Sometimes Googles "things to do near Arnold CA this weekend." Has used GoCalaveras.com once or twice.

**Relationship to Hwy4Events:** Miguel is a casual, infrequent visitor. He doesn't bookmark sites or check regularly. He'll find Hwy4Events via Google search or a shared link. First impressions are everything — he'll decide in 5 seconds if this site is useful or if he should go back to Yelp.

**Jobs to be done:**
- "We're thinking about driving up Saturday — is anything happening?"
- Quick gut check: is it worth the 90-minute drive?
- Find something specific to anchor the trip (a festival, a concert, a tasting event)
- Logistics: where exactly is this? How long does it take? What else is nearby?

**Pain points:**
- Doesn't know the geography — "Arnold" and "Murphys" mean nothing to him. Needs context.
- If the site doesn't show up in Google results, it doesn't exist
- Wants to see enough events to justify the drive — a single Thursday lecture won't do it
- Needs to quickly assess: "Is this a real thing or some small community meeting?"

**Design implications:**
- SEO is the acquisition channel for Miguel — the site must rank for "things to do near Arnold CA"
- Map view with town context helps orient a visitor who doesn't know the area
- Event "size" or "vibe" indicators help him filter (festival vs. book club)
- "This Weekend" is his only view — he doesn't care about next Thursday
- Cross-linking to Google Maps for directions
- OG/social sharing images matter — if someone shares an event on Instagram, it needs to look good

## Design Principles (Derived from Personas)

1. **Speed over chrome.** Gary, Dave, and Miguel will all bounce if the site is slow. Performance is a feature.

2. **Mobile-first, always.** Every persona except Jen (Sunday laptop planning) is on their phone. Design for thumb-scrolling.

3. **No gates.** No sign-up walls, no app installs, no notification prompts. Earn repeat visits through usefulness.

4. **"This Weekend" is the killer view.** 5 of 7 personas enter through a weekend lens. Make it the default or prominently featured.

5. **Trust is built on accuracy.** Stale, wrong, or missing events destroy credibility with every persona. Data freshness is existential.

6. **Shareable by default.** Gary shares with his buddy. Rob shares with Vivian. Karen shares with guests. Miguel's friend shares on Instagram. Every event should have a clean, shareable URL with good OG images.

7. **Local voice, not corporate voice.** Mia will ignore it if it sounds like a tourism board. Gary will ignore it if it sounds like AI. The voice should sound like a neighbor who happens to know everything going on.
