# Prompt: Hwy 4 Corridor Local Knowledge Research

Use this prompt in Claude.ai (with web search enabled) to build a comprehensive local knowledge base for hwy4events.com.

---

## The Prompt

I'm building a hyperlocal events and community site for the Highway 4 corridor in Calaveras and Alpine counties, California. The corridor runs roughly from Copperopolis through Angels Camp, Murphys, Arnold, Dorrington, and up to Bear Valley.

I have 7 user personas representing the real people who live in, visit, and do business along this corridor. I need you to research and compile the **deep local knowledge** each of these personas would carry — the kind of thing you'd only know if you lived there, worked there, or spent serious time there.

For each persona below, research and document:

### Categories to cover for each persona:

1. **Daily routine places** — Where do they get coffee, breakfast, lunch, dinner? Where do they grab groceries, gas, hardware? Be specific: real business names, real towns.

2. **Social gathering spots** — Bars, breweries, tasting rooms, cafes, lodges, community centers where they'd run into people they know. Include the vibe (dive bar vs. wine bar vs. family spot).

3. **Local vocabulary & geography** — Words and phrases locals use that outsiders wouldn't know. Examples: "Ebbetts Pass," "the 4," "BLS" (Blue Lake Springs), "up the hill," "down the hill," "the valley" (Central Valley), "Gold Country." Town nicknames, road references, seasonal language ("chain control," "burn day").

4. **Known local figures & proprietors** — Real business owners, community leaders, and personalities that are part of the social fabric. Examples I already know: Bonnie and Craig at Hinterhaus Distillery in Arnold, the folks at Snowshoe Brewery. Who else? Winery owners in Murphys, restaurant owners, lodge leaders, event organizers, the people who ARE the community.

5. **Institutions & organizations** — Moose Lodge, Lions Club, Blue Lake Springs HOA, Calaveras County Fair, Bear Valley Mountain Resort, Mark Twain Medical Center, volunteer fire departments, school districts, churches that host events. Which ones matter to which personas?

6. **Seasonal rhythms** — What happens when? Ski season at Bear Valley, wine crush season in Murphys, summer tourism, Jumping Frog Jubilee, holiday events, shoulder seasons when it's dead quiet. How does each persona experience the calendar differently?

7. **Media & information channels** — Calaveras Enterprise newspaper, MyMotherLode.com, Facebook groups (which specific ones?), Instagram accounts worth following, Nextdoor communities, email lists, flyers-on-the-bulletin-board culture. Where does each persona actually get their information?

8. **Pain points about the area** — What frustrates each persona? Limited cell service in certain spots? The drive time from the Bay Area? Restaurant hours being unpredictable? Snow closures? Lack of late-night options? Things being "cash only"?

9. **Hidden gems & local favorites** — The places and experiences that don't show up on Yelp or Google but that locals swear by. The swimming hole, the back road, the Tuesday special, the guy who does firewood delivery.

10. **Relationships between towns** — How do locals think about the towns relative to each other? Murphys is "wine country" / "bougie." Arnold is "the real mountain community." Dorrington is "quiet." Angels Camp is "down the hill" and more commercial. Bear Valley is "up top" and seasonal. Copperopolis is "not really the mountains." What are the tribal identities?

---

### The 7 Personas:

**1. Gary (68) — Plugged-In Retiree, Arnold**
Full-time Arnold resident, retired teacher from Sacramento. iPhone-comfortable but prefers mobile web. Already wired into the local grapevine — Moose Lodge, Calaveras Enterprise, post office flyers, Arnold Byway Cafe. His wife is in multiple Facebook groups. Research his world: the retiree social circuit, the places he'd go daily, the people he'd know by first name.

**2. Mia (27) — Young Winery Worker, Murphys**
Tasting room associate at a Murphys winery. Moved from Fresno 2 years ago. Instagram-first, doesn't use Facebook. Knows Murphys Main Street cold but has no idea what happens in Arnold or Bear Valley. Research her world: the young service-industry social scene, where she goes after work, which Murphys businesses she'd interact with daily, the Instagram accounts she follows.

**3. Dave (45) — Dorrington Contractor**
Runs a small contracting business, lifelong corridor resident. Moderate tech comfort. Hears about things from clients and at the hardware store. Goes to car shows, BBQ competitions, community fundraisers. His wife handles the social calendar. Research his world: the blue-collar corridor life, where contractors eat lunch, the hardware stores and lumber yards, the community events that draw working men.

**4. Rob (48) — Bay Area Weekend Visitor**
Lives in Los Gatos, comes up about monthly. CSO at a media tech company. Very high tech comfort. Plans weekends around events and dining. Research his world: the visitor experience, the drive up, the restaurants a Bay Area foodie would seek out, the wineries, the "what do you do on a Saturday in Murphys" question.

**5. Karen (52) — Absentee Airbnb Owner, Arnold**
Lives in Walnut Creek, owns 4 Airbnb units in Arnold. Former real estate agent. Visits quarterly. Needs to advise guests on what to do. Research her world: the short-term rental ecosystem, what Airbnb guests ask about, the "welcome guide" recommendations, the property management community.

**6. Jen (38) — Blue Lake Springs Mom, Arnold**
Full-time Arnold resident, moved from Bay Area during COVID. Kids ages 4 and 9. Active on Facebook groups, Nextdoor, BLS community portal. Research her world: the young-family Arnold experience, kid-friendly activities, the school system, BLS amenities and social life, the "transplant from the Bay Area" community.

**7. Miguel (34) — Central Valley Day-Tripper**
Lives in Stockton, drives up sporadically. Discovers things via Instagram and Google. Doesn't know the geography well. Research his world: the day-tripper experience, what Google surfaces for "things to do near Arnold CA," the drive from Stockton, first-timer confusion, what makes someone come back.

---

### Output format:

For each persona, give me a structured breakdown across all 10 categories. Be specific — I want real business names, real locations, real Facebook group names, real event names. Where you're uncertain, flag it as [VERIFY] so I can ground-truth it locally.

After the 7 persona sections, add a **"Corridor-Wide Knowledge Base"** section that compiles:
- Master list of businesses by town (restaurants, bars, shops, services)
- Master list of organizations and institutions
- Annual event calendar
- Local vocabulary glossary
- Key community figures
- Media/information source directory

This will become the foundational knowledge base for an AI-powered local events site that needs to sound like a neighbor, not a tourism board.
